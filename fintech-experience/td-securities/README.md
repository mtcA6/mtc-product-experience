## Transformation: 

Every banking experience I've had has been about some sort of transformation. Sometimes it's a technical transformation where I've supported teams shifting from a waterfall (Morgan Stanley) or a project based approach (Sandy Spring) to use Agile values and methodologies. Every banking role I've had we've had some sort of Lending Transformation where we've done things like changed the application experience to build a digital first application for small businesses or an internal digital transformation to make use of custom built applications to buy, sell, and securitize loans. Those transformations had a lot of buzz. Now, of course, everyone is talking about AI transformations and the buzz is a deafening roar. This mini analysis explores my experience supporting an AI transformation. 

## Need for speed... or training?

While we're constantly transforming from one 'stagnant' tool or methodology to the next, what remains the same is always a need to make shared understanding of the process, tools, strategies, ideas, and taboos for whatever adventure we're on. 

There's absolutely a need for, what I'm being drawn to call, an AI Literacy. Learning the basic tools is key and its important for employeers to train employees on the new tools they're demanding we use. Fortunately for me TD Securities offered an AI Bootcamp.

## Starting up windows, or in this case AI

We've had a few main gaps to navigate, access and training. 

### Access
> "You can lead a horse to water but you cannot make it drink."

At the outset, we had to request specialized access to Copilot. Once we had access, leadership was constantly hounding us to make use of the AI tools we had to experiement and to amend our working strategies to leverage AI whenever and wherever possible. 

With the initial access gap resolved, the dabbling began. I started watching youtube videos about prompting and strategies for implementing AI. The results were honestly lackluster. I felt like some of the formatting issues and the content weren't hitting the mark so my adoption was shoddy at first. 

Access was the water but without the training we weren't sure how to drink. 

### Training

For months now, we've had a reocurring meeting scheduled where teams share their AI experiences and demo strategies they've applied and the outcomes that resulted by showing the product/output, or they go the more classic powerpoint deck showing some of their prompts and talking about what they've built. This was good and all, but I was missing something. 

I've been fortunate to be a member of a development team that was tagged to join pilot group for TD Securities' AI Transformation. Being a part of the bootcamp was really motivational. It wasn't a 30 second video on youtube saying "You're a stupid product person with no dev skills, but now you can make an app.", instead it was a multi-day bootcamp where we had real, hands on exercises and training. 

After the bootcamp, we then had even more practical hands on engagement. This training for me looked like 10 weeks with 3 additional resources embedded with my team. They joined every ceremony I led during the engagement period. Having colleagues embeded with my team, who trained to train other people, to coach them in strategies for prompting was incredibly helpful. It felt like earlier days of my career where peers and managers helped coach me as I learned to navigate the corporate world. 

As a product person, I immediately put this to use building tickets. You learn pretty quickly 2 things: 

1. AI needs context
2. AI writes too many words

## What still works 

What has become abundantly clear over the past 6 months is that no matter what the tool training and collaboration are still infinitely more valuable than an independently developed self-directed learning program. Sharing strategies in an explicit and formally structured way over an extended period of time was invaluable. 



## graveyard 

No matter the type of transformation, adoption isn't uniform, and training is key. 

Our tickets may look more complete now that I'm using AI, but if we're not careful a ticket that a human could understand using one sentnece, they're facing fatigue at the end of the 4 paragraphs the AI easily vomits.
